Chronicle

Your character's story, written as you play.

Everything you would look up on a website, Chronicle learns as you go: who has work for you, where a foe lives, what it drops, who was with you in the Deadmines. You build your own book of the world, and it only knows what your character has seen.

As you play

A one-line notice comes up as things happen, and a ring around its picture fills as a quest's steps get done. Point at one to read more; click it to go where it leads. A new place or a new foe gets a few words of its own beneath. Move them, size them, stack them, or turn off any kind you don't want.

2 of 8 Kobold Vermin slain for Investigate Echo Ridge. You took on Book Collector in Elwynn Forest. Mangy Wolf was added to your Bestiary. You completed Book Collector in Elwynn Forest.

Take the tour

Chronicle is one window with a tab for each part of your records, and every highlighted name leads somewhere: a place to the Atlas, a dungeon to its page in the Journey, a companion to everything you've done together.

Hints that grow as you do

When your character is ready for a dungeon or the next step of their class training, the Journey opens a chapter for it. Its hint starts vague and fills in as you explore and ask around.

At firstSomeone out in the world may know of work in the Deadmines.
Once you've been to WestfallSomeone in Westfall may know of work in the Deadmines.
After a word with an innkeeperGryan Stoutmantle, in Sentinel Hill, may know of work in the Deadmines.

Then the dungeon's page follows you through it: its quests, its bosses and every run you've made. No spoilers: a step stays hidden until you reach it.

Every foe, in your character's words

…and the Bestiary opens on every foe that has dropped it. You're in Westfall, so the search keeps to Westfall's foes first.

Each foe has what your race and class make of it, field notes from what you've seen of it, what it dropped and how often, and where you fought it.

Maps that fill in as you explore

Click Westfall under Fought in, and the Atlas map lights up where you fought the Defias Trapper. Set a waypoint there and go.

Each zone keeps the foes you fought there, what you gathered and the quests you did, and a herb or ore leads to the zones you gathered it in.

The people you play with

Everyone you've grouped with, most often first: the parties, dungeon runs, quests handed in and foes of note you brought down together.

Your fellowship grows as you play together, from Acquaintance to Sworn. Search for a dungeon, a zone or a foe to see who was with you.

What sets it apart

Your race writes it

A dwarf sounds like a dwarf and a troll like a troll, for every race and class, Skyborne included.

It never changes how you play

No quest arrows, no rotation helpers, no nagging. It writes down what you did.

It stays out of the way

Notices are one slim line each, where you put them. You choose which kinds you see, down to a sale at a merchant.

A new level is an occasion

A seal with your class crest steps up to your new level with the game's own sound, then your attributes count up and your trainer's new skills are listed.

No spoilers

It only knows what your character has seen or been told.

Easy on your game

It keeps what matters and lets the routine go, so your records stay small and quick.

The little things

Chronicle writes down what you kill, loot, skin, gather, make, trade and finish, and each tab reads from the others. It knows only what you've done, so every number in it comes from your own play.

Search by what drops

Type a thing's name in the Atlas and it lists the foes that dropped it, zone by zone. In Professions, the same search lists every recipe that takes it.

Who was there

Search Companions for a dungeon, a zone or a foe to see who was with you.

What killed you

A death names the foe. Swear vengeance on it right from the notice, and the Bestiary keeps count of how many you have killed since.

Dungeon loot

Each boss you killed has a Bestiary page with what it dropped for you. Blue or better from a dungeon's other foes is kept too, under "Other foes".

All your characters

Tick "Show all character data" and your other characters' foes, maps and recipes show beside this one's, faded.

Trades and merchants

What changed hands, any money, and with whom; what you sold, bought and paid for repairs.

The same line, three voices

Every line is written once, and your race speaks it its own way.

There is always one more place over the next rise, and I'm beginning to enjoy that.
